\section*{Introduction}
    \begin{itemize}
    \item Terrestrial ecosystems regulate global \textbf{carbon, water, and energy balances}; forest functioning is closely linked to vegetation structure and functional traits.

    \item Increasing \textbf{drought, heat, and biotic disturbances} are causing widespread tree mortality, with potentially large consequences for ecosystem functioning.

    \item Mortality alters \textbf{canopy structure and leaf area}, affecting carbon uptake, evapotranspiration, and energy partitioning, with cascading effects on soils, nutrients, and vegetation recovery.

    \item The magnitude and trajectory of these impacts likely depend on \textbf{vegetation type and functional trait composition}.

    \item However, quantitative understanding of mortality impacts across ecosystems remains limited due to \textbf{spatial and temporal constraints of field observations} and limited integration of mortality and trait data.

    \item Advances in \textbf{remote sensing, machine learning, citizen science, and trait databases} now enable large-scale assessments of mortality and ecosystem responses.

    \item This creates an opportunity to quantify how mortality affects ecosystem functioning and identify \textbf{vegetation types that are more sensitive or resilient}.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{What changed in this version}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Dataset:} deadtrees v5 mortality maps (delivered 2026-10-06).
  \item \textbf{Main learner: AutoGluon} (AutoML ensemble). XGBoost and LightGBM (Optuna-tuned) are in the Appendix as a check.

\end{itemize}

\textbf{Table~\ref{tab:main}: Does mortality reduce the prediction error?} (MAE, site-year level, paired bootstrap)
\begin{itemize}
  \item Every site-year is an observation, so years without mortality do not dilute the years with mortality. Years of
        one site are not independent, so whole sites are resampled (bootstrap) and swapped (paired permutation).
  \item \textbf{NEP$_{max}$: yes, robustly.} MAE $-11.8$ / $-10.8$\,\% over all site-years (12 / 24\,months), $-17.5$ to
        $-22.6$\,\% at High sites and \textbf{$-29$ to $-32$\,\% in high-mortality years}; significant in 7 of 8 cells.
  \item \textbf{WUE: only where mortality happened.} $-4$ to $-5$\,\% over all site-years (n.s.), but $-11$ to $-15$\,\% at
        High sites and $-17$ to $-22$\,\% in high-mortality years, all significant.
  \item \textbf{GPP$_{sat}$: smaller} ($-4$ to $-12$\,\%), significant over all site-years at 24\,months and in the
        Mid + High years; at High sites and in High years the intervals are wide (few site-years).
  \item \textbf{ET$_{max}$: small} ($-1$ to $-11$\,\%), significant only in the Mid + High years at 24\,months.
  \item \textbf{The effect is largest in the years when mortality happened}: for every EFP the error reduction in High
        years is at least twice the one over all site-years (NEP$_{max}$ about 2.7 times).
  \item The bootstrap intervals and the permutation tests agree: every significant cell has its 95\,\% interval entirely
        below zero.
  \item Robustness (Appendix): site-level tests (per-site MAE; log-ratio permutation and site-level bootstrap), RMSE
        instead of MAE, XGBoost and LightGBM, sites weighted by their number of years, one-year sites dropped. NEP$_{max}$
        improves in all of them.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Figure 3: Does the direction differ between High and Low+Mid sites?} (signed SHAP)
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Below zero means the model predicts a LOWER value of the EFP} (not that it predicts less accurately).
        Classes: site maximum of total mortality; High $>$ mean + 0.5\,SD (22 High vs 79 Low+Mid). Mann--Whitney U,
        BH-FDR across the figure.
  \item \textbf{A clear dose--response.} At \textbf{High}-mortality sites the block pushes predicted carbon uptake
        \emph{down}; at \textbf{Low+Mid} sites slightly \emph{up} (relative to the average site).
  \item GPP$_{sat}$: High $-1.83$ / $-2.15$, Low+Mid $+0.47$ / $+0.66$ $\mu$mol\,m$^{-2}$\,s$^{-1}$ (12 / 24\,m);

        $+0.07$ g\,C\,mm$^{-1}$; ET$_{max}$: tiny ($-0.003$ / $-0.005$\,mm\,d$^{-1}$). All 8 tests $q<0.001$.
  \item \textbf{Read with Table~\ref{tab:main}:} mortality improves accuracy by lowering predictions at damaged sites.
        Sites are grouped by the same variables whose contribution is measured, so the test confirms that the learned
        relationship is consistent; the direction and size are the informative part.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Figure 4: The direction tracks total mortality}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Per site, net signed mortality SHAP against the site's maximum total mortality (AutoGluon, C+T+M, 24\,months);
        linear fit with 95\,\% CI, Spearman test.
  \item \textbf{Strong and negative for the carbon fluxes and WUE:} $\rho$ = $-0.81$ (GPP$_{sat}$), $-0.83$ (NEP$_{max}$),
        $-0.78$ (WUE); weaker for ET$_{max}$ ($-0.47$); all $p<0.001$. More mortality, lower predicted carbon uptake.
  \item Sites with little mortality get a positive push, so the sign changes at roughly 10--20\,\% total mortality.
  \item The benefit itself (error reduction) does not depend on tree cover ($\rho$ = 0.10--0.18, n.s.), and the direction
        tracks mortality more closely than ecosystem type or tree cover (full board in the Appendix).
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Figure 5: Without mortality the model predicts too high in high-mortality years}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The Low / Mid / High site groups lose power (22 High sites). Here every \textbf{site-year} is an observation, grouped
        by the total mortality of that year; y = mean residual (observed $-$ predicted), below 0 = the model predicts too high.
  \item \textbf{Without mortality (C+T)} the model over-predicts in the $>$30\,\% class (21 site-years, 10 sites):
        NEP$_{max}$ by $-4.0$\,$\mu$mol\,m$^{-2}$\,s$^{-1}$ [$-5.5$, $-2.4$], GPP$_{sat}$ by $-4.3$ [$-7.5$, $-1.3$],
        WUE by $-0.77$\,g\,C\,mm$^{-1}$ [$-1.10$, $-0.29$] (AutoGluon, 24\,months; 95\,\% site-block bootstrap).
        In the low classes the residual is close to 0.
  \item \textbf{With mortality (C+T+M)} the over-prediction shrinks by about two-thirds (NEP$_{max}$ $-1.3$, GPP$_{sat}$
        $-1.1$, WUE $-0.43$) and the intervals include 0. ET$_{max}$ shows no pattern.
  \item \textbf{The correction is significant.} Per site-year, correction = residual with $-$ residual without mortality
        (= how much adding mortality lowers the prediction). In the $>$30\,\% class: GPP$_{sat}$ +3.2, NEP$_{max}$ +2.7,
        WUE +0.34 (all $q<0.01$, paired permutation with sites swapped as blocks); in the 0--10\,\% class mortality
        slightly \emph{raises} the predictions ($-0.8$, $-0.7$, $-0.10$). The correction grows with total mortality
        (mixed model, per +10\,\%: GPP$_{sat}$ +0.96, NEP$_{max}$ +0.75, WUE +0.10; all $q<0.001$, site-block bootstrap).
        XGBoost and LightGBM: the same, all significant, with smaller corrections (about half; Appendix~A7).
  \item \textbf{Mixed model on the residuals} (residual $\sim$ total mortality of the year, random intercept per site; all
        361 site-years): without mortality the residual falls by $-0.77$ (NEP$_{max}$), $-0.18$ (WUE) and $-0.59$
        (GPP$_{sat}$) per +10\,\% total mortality; robust to a site-block bootstrap, to leaving out any single site and to
        removing the three sites with $>$50\,\% total mortality. With mortality the trend is no longer significant (Appendix~A7).
  \item The result comes mostly from the few site-years with $>$30\,\% total mortality --- the years in which mortality happened.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Mortality metrics}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The mortality block contains five metrics (absolute deadwood, relative deadwood, deadwood gain, relative
        tree-cover loss, total mortality) at five buffers. They are strongly correlated and largely interchangeable:
        removing any single metric changes the error by at most about 1\,\% (drop-column test, Appendix).
  \item \textbf{Total mortality carries most of the effect} (added alone to C+T it gives 6.7--7.1\,\% of the 7.6--8.9\,\%
        NEP$_{max}$ error reduction of the full block) and has the largest SHAP share (26\,\%), so the figures use total
        mortality only. The other metrics are in the Appendix.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Methods}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Dataset:} deadtrees v5 mortality maps, FLUXNET EFPs (GPP$_{sat}$, NEP$_{max}$ following
        Migliavacca et al.\ 2021, ET$_{max}$, WUE), Lusk et al.\ 2025 trait maps (1\,km). Tree cover $\geq$30\,\%
        (500\,m, site mean), EFPs in runs of $\geq$2 consecutive years, CN-Sb1/2 removed by QC:
        \textbf{101 sites, 361 site-years}.
  \item \textbf{Models:} \textbf{climate + traits (C+T)} vs \textbf{climate + traits + mortality (C+T+M)}.
        The pair differs only by the mortality block, so it isolates its contribution.
  \item \textbf{Learner: AutoGluon-Tabular 1.6} (default model zoo: LightGBM, CatBoost, XGBoost, random forest, extra
        trees, two neural networks; weighted ensemble; 5-fold bagging on site-disjoint folds; no stacking). AutoGluon
        is refitted inside every cross-validation fold, so model selection never sees the held-out site. The ensemble
        is dominated by the two neural networks (mean weights 0.43 and 0.32).
  \item \textbf{Check learners (Appendix):} XGBoost and LightGBM, each tuned with Optuna (TPE, 50 trials per
        response and window, grouped 5-fold CV on disjoint site groups).
  \item \textbf{Windows:} climate strictly before the growing season --- 12 months (lag 1) or 24 months (lag 1 + lag 2)
        ending the month before season start; mortality of the current year plus lag 1 (12\,m) or lag 1 + lag 2 (24\,m).
  \item \textbf{Cross-validation:} repeated leave-one-site-out (3 $\times$ 80\,\% of the remaining sites per
        held-out site; predictions averaged over the three repetitions).
  \item \textbf{Mortality predictors:} absolute deadwood, relative deadwood, deadwood gain, relative tree-cover loss
        and total mortality, each at five buffers (100, 200, 300, 400, 500\,m) and the lags above.
  \item \textbf{Site classes} in the figures: site maximum of total mortality across years, mean of the five buffers;
        breaks mean $\pm$ 0.5\,SD (Low $<$ 10.6\,\%, High $>$ 24.0\,\%; 41 Low, 38 Mid, 22 High).
  \item \textbf{Mortality metrics} (from the deadtrees v5 deadwood / tree-cover maps, per buffer):
        \begin{itemize}
          \item \textbf{Absolute deadwood} = standing deadwood / buffer area $\times$ 100 (\%).
          \item \textbf{Relative deadwood} = standing deadwood / tree cover $\times$ 100 (\%), where tree cover $\geq$ 10\,\%, capped at 100\,\%.
          \item \textbf{Total mortality} = (deadwood + tree-cover loss) / (tree cover + tree-cover loss) $\times$ 100 (\%):
                standing dead and removed canopy relative to the canopy present.
        \end{itemize}
  \item \textbf{SHAP:} permutation SHAP for AutoGluon (model-agnostic, approximate); exact TreeSHAP for XGBoost and
        LightGBM. Compare shares and signs between learners, not magnitudes.
  \item \textbf{Statistics:}
        \begin{itemize}
          \item \emph{Main test} (Table~\ref{tab:main}): paired bootstrap at the site-year level --- every site-year is an
                observation, statistic = pooled MAE of C+T+M minus pooled MAE of C+T (\% = ratio of the pooled errors).
                Sites resampled with replacement with all their years (10\,000 draws, 95\,\% percentile interval);
                paired permutation test with the C+T / C+T+M labels swapped per site (10\,000 draws, one-sided).
          \item \emph{Site level} (Appendix): per-site MAE over the site's years; site-level bootstrap and a one-sided paired
                sign-flip permutation test on the per-site log error ratios (100\,000 flips; geometric-mean change).
          \item \emph{Dose--response} (Fig.~5): mean residual per class of the site-year's total mortality with a site-block
                bootstrap (2000 draws); correction = residual(C+T+M) $-$ residual(C+T), one-sided paired permutation with
                the two models swapped per site (10\,000 draws); mixed models (nlme) residual or correction $\sim$ total
                mortality + (1\,|\,site), slopes checked with a site-block bootstrap, leave-one-site-out and without sites
                $>$50\,\% total mortality.
          \item Fig.~3: Mann--Whitney U. Fig.~4: Spearman. Benjamini--Hochberg FDR within each table / figure
                (tables: across all three learners).
        \end{itemize}
  \item Interactive report with all learners and datasets:\\
        \url{https://negin-katal.github.io/fluxVSmortality/v10/index.html}
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{A6. Mortality metrics}
\begin{itemize}

        24\,\%, relative tree-cover loss 22\,\%, absolute deadwood 16\,\%, deadwood gain 12\,\%; for ET$_{max}$ tree-cover
        loss leads (32\,\%).
  \item Drop-column test (XGBoost and LightGBM retrained without each metric, or with only one metric added to C+T):
        removing any single metric changes the error by at most about 1\,\%, because the correlated metrics take over
        (total mortality vs relative deadwood $\rho$ = 0.91). Absolute deadwood gets 14--16\,\% of the SHAP but adds
        nothing when removed.

\subsection*{A7. Fig.~5: other learners, 12 months, mixed models and robustness}
\begin{itemize}
  \item XGBoost and LightGBM over-predict in the $>$30\,\% class as much as AutoGluon without mortality (NEP$_{max}$ $-3.6$ to
        $-4.0$\,$\mu$mol\,m$^{-2}$\,s$^{-1}$); with mortality the over-prediction is smaller but stays below 0 ($-1.8$ to $-2.4$).
        Their correction in the $>$30\,\% class is significant for GPP$_{sat}$ (+1.4 to +1.8), NEP$_{max}$ (+1.2 to +2.2)
        and WUE (+0.12 to +0.19), all $q<0.05$.
  \item Robustness of the mixed-model slopes (Fig.~A21): mixed model, site-block bootstrap (1000 draws), leave one site out
        (range over 101 refits) and without the three most extreme sites (FI-Hyy, US-GLE, US-NC2). The over-prediction
        without mortality (middle row) holds in every check for NEP$_{max}$ and WUE; with mortality (bottom row) no check is
        significant for GPP$_{sat}$, NEP$_{max}$ or WUE. Whether the error reduction itself grows with mortality (top row)
        goes the same way but is not robust in the bootstrap.
\end{itemize}
